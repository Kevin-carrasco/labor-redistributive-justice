# A Fair Share? Labour Market Position, Perceived Technological Risk and Redistributive Justice across Welfare Regimes in 27 OECD Countries

Replication materials and writing environment for the manuscript listed above.
The paper is written in Quarto and the analyses run in R.

**Status:** manuscript under peer review (Journal of European Social Policy).
Contents may change in response to the review process.

## Overview

The study examines perceived redistributive justice, understood as the sense of
receiving a fair share of public benefits given the taxes and contributions one
pays. It uses the 2024 OECD *Risks That Matter* (RTM) survey and estimates
multilevel ordinal logistic regression models (`ordinal::clmm`, survey weights,
random intercept by country) for 19,669 workers in 27 OECD countries, with
country-level covariates and welfare regime as a level-2 moderator.
Hypotheses were preregistered on OSF.

## Data

| Source | File | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| OECD Risks That Matter 2024 | `input/original/OECD_RTM_2024.dta` | Main analytic source |
| World Bank / OECD indicators | retrieved via `WDI` in `procesamiento/` | Gini, GDP per capita, unemployment, social expenditure |

Raw RTM microdata are not redistributed in this repository. They are obtained
directly from the OECD under its access conditions and must be placed in
`input/original/` with the file names above before running the pipeline. The
questionnaires are included for documentation:
`OECD_Risks_That_Matter_2024_Core_Questionnaire.pdf` and
`..._Background_Questionnaire.pdf`.

**Analytic sample.** Respondents who are employed or temporarily absent from
work (`s10` = 1, 4, 5). Job seekers and those not seeking work (`s10` = 2, 3) are
excluded because the questionnaire routing does not administer them the
self-employment, platform work and technological change items. The restriction
is therefore structural and is reported as the main limitation of the study.

├── 05-discussion.qmd
├── 06-conclusion.qmd

├── references.qmd

│   ├── procesamiento.qmd      # Data preparation: recoding, scales, country data
